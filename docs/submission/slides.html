The problem

Everyone knows who owes them. Nobody makes the call.

Collections is uncomfortable, repetitive, and never urgent enough to do today. So the invoice ages, and the cash gap widens.

The product

One call. Two acceptable endings.

Promise to pay

The caller names a date. The agent confirms day, month and year back, then records it with a reference number.

Pay now

The caller wants to settle. The agent issues a one-time payment link and stops talking. The link is mocked in this demo.

It confirms who is speaking, looks the invoice up before it will discuss a number, and keeps every reply to a sentence or two.

  • Keyterms so invoice numbers and company names transcribe correctly

  • Turn detection tuned to wait rather than talk over an annoyed caller

Business case

Sold to the person who makes the call today

  • Buyer: owner or bookkeeper at a small B2B services firm, chasing 10 to 50 invoices a month
  • Alternative today: do it themselves, or hand the debt to an agency
  • Pricing: per recovered invoice, or a flat monthly fee well under an agency's cut
  • Why voice: email is ignored; a call gets a named person and a date
  • Proven market: Peakflo, Resolve Pay and Thoughtly already sell AI collection calls to larger firms

Scope and next

What this is not, and what comes next

Out of scope

In this demo
